## Shape-transfer analysis extended with two credit_life data points

With both 877 and 878 complete (same `structural_variant: credit_life`,
scraped under identical Limited/PPT10/8% assumptions), ran three
comparisons:

1. **877 vs 878 (same family, same payment-option assumptions)**: 0.1-2.7%
   deviation across every point checked - as tight as 875-vs-876's earlier
   result. Their absolute premiums also sit in a near-constant ~1.24-1.28x
   ratio at every point (877 consistently pricier than 878) - a pure level
   shift, not a shape difference.
2. **877 vs 876/875 (different family)**: 9-19% divergence, widening with
   age (9% at age 30, 17-19% at age 45).
3. **878 vs 876/875 (different family, no payment-option confound relative
   to 877's own comparison)**: 15-19.6% divergence at age 45, closely
   matching 877's own divergence magnitude (877: -19.3%/-18.3%,
   878: -17.6%/-16.0%) despite 877 and 878 differing in sale channel
   (offline vs online) and branding (Yuva vs Digi).

**Key inference: since 877 and 878 both diverge from 876/875 by nearly the
same amount despite using identical payment-option assumptions to each
other, this divergence is very unlikely to be primarily a Limited-vs-Regular
payment-structure artifact - it looks like a real, repeatable
`structural_variant` (credit_life vs standalone) effect.** Still can't fully
rule out a general Regular-vs-Limited effect (no regular-premium credit_life
data exists to test that in isolation), but two independent credit_life
products landing on nearly the same divergence number is real evidence, not
noise.

**Updated summary table** (logged in `docs/query_architecture.md`'s open
questions):

| Pair | Divergence at oldest age checked | Same structural_variant? |
|---|---|---|
| 875 vs 876 | 0.6-2.4% | Yes (standalone) |
| 877 vs 878 | 0.1-2.7% | Yes (credit_life) |
| 877/878 vs 876/875 | 15-19% | No |
| 876 vs 859 | 30-33% | No, and different regulatory template too |

Suggestive pattern: divergence scales with how different the two policies'
families actually are - same-family pairs transfer tightly regardless of
other differences (channel, branding, absolute price level); cross-family
divergence is moderate when only `structural_variant` differs
(credit_life vs standalone) and much larger when the products are also from
different regulatory templates (859 is a standardized cross-insurer product).

## Session 2: 954's spot-check, and the divergence pattern refined into a graduated spectrum

954 (New Tech-Term, `rider_enabled`) - all 7 points scraped (regular premium,
online, SA 50L - 954's own `sum_assured_min`; no free brochure cross-check
available at this reference SA, since 954's own brochure sample table uses a
1Cr baseline instead). Saved to `954_scraped_premiums.csv`.

**954 vs 876/875: 4.7-7.5% divergence at age 45** - tighter than 877/878's
15-19% credit_life gap, but looser than 875-vs-876's 0.6-2.4%. This is a
genuinely useful third data point: `rider_enabled` (954) sits structurally
between `standalone` (875/876) and `credit_life` (877/878) in terms of curve
divergence, which lines up with intuition - a rider-enabled product is still
a flat-cover term plan at its base (same death-benefit mechanism as
standalone), just with optional add-ons, unlike credit_life's genuinely
different decreasing-cover pricing.

**954 vs 859 (both share the wide 18-65 age range): 20.8-23.4% divergence at
age 45** - larger than 954-vs-876/875, smaller than 876-vs-859's 30-33%,
again consistent with 954 sitting "in between."

**Full ordering now established**: 875≈876 (tightest, 0.6-2.4%) < 954-vs-
876/875 (4.7-7.5%) < 877≈878 (tightest cross-check, credit_life-vs-
credit_life) < 877/878-vs-876/875 (15-19%) < 954-vs-859 (20.8-23.4%) <
876-vs-859 (30-33%, largest). Updated the summary table and the pattern
description in `docs/query_architecture.md`'s open questions: divergence
doesn't just correlate with same/different `structural_variant`, it appears
to graduate by *degree* of structural difference, and involving 859 (a
different regulatory template, not just a different `structural_variant`)

regardless of which policy 859 is compared against.

## Session 3: 955's spot-check completes the original plan, and the shape-transfer pattern is now solidly confirmed across all 3 structural_variant families

955 (New Jeevan Amar, `rider_enabled` - same variant as 954) - all 7 points
scraped (regular premium, SA 25L - 955's own `sum_assured_min`, half of
954's reference). One real discrepancy caught mid-scrape: the live
calculator used `distribution_channel: online` for all 7 quotes, but 955's
own brochure explicitly states "This Plan can be purchased Offline through
Licensed agents..." - confirmed by re-checking the PDF directly, not assumed
away. Recorded what the calculator actually produced (online) rather than
overriding to match the brochure, flagged clearly in the CSV's notes and
committed separately - the calculator's real output is the ground truth for
what this data source can give us, the brochure/tool mismatch itself is the
finding, not something to paper over.

**955 vs 954 (same `structural_variant: rider_enabled`): 0.7-2.0% divergence,
holding even out to age 60** - the tightest same-family match of any pair
checked so far, tighter than 875-876 and comparable to 877-878.

**955 vs 876/875: 5.2-6.8% divergence** - landing almost exactly on 954's own
4.7-7.5% divergence from the same pair, despite 955 and 954 differing in SA
reference (25L vs 50L) and (per the discrepancy above) distribution channel.
**955 vs 859: 23.1-24.2%** - again nearly identical to 954's 20.8-23.4%.

**This is now a solidly confirmed pattern, not a suggestive one**: all 3
`structural_variant` families in this corpus (standalone: 875/876,
credit_life: 877/878, rider_enabled: 954/955) show tight same-family
transfer (0.1-2.7%) and cross-family divergence that graduates by *degree*
of structural difference - `rider_enabled` vs `standalone` mildest
(4.7-7.5%), `credit_life` vs `standalone` larger (15-19%), any comparison
involving 859 (different regulatory template, not just a different
`structural_variant`) largest (20.8-33%). Two independent `rider_enabled`
products (954, 955) landing on nearly identical divergence numbers against
the same comparison policies, despite differing in SA/channel from each
other, is real evidence this is a structural effect and not coincidence.
Rewrote the summary table and surrounding analysis in
`docs/query_architecture.md`'s open questions to reflect this - now stated
as a genuinely useful practical rule for scoping future policies' scraping
effort, not just a research finding.
