## Design: two independent axes, not one interpolation formula

Confirmed (visualized first, via an artifact plotting the 68 real scraped points -
premium vs. age by term, and SA-scaling multiplier by age band) that premium
separates cleanly into two axes that need different treatment:

- **age x term:** genuinely non-linear (mortality cost visibly accelerates with
  age in the chart) - handled via **bilinear interpolation** over the real
  (age, term) points we have, at one reference sum_assured per policy (876's own
  Rs 50L baseline - also its `sum_assured_min`, so 0% rebate applies there).
  Outside the known grid's convex hull -> excluded, never extrapolated, matching
  the existing exclude-and-log behavior for out-of-range candidates elsewhere in
  `premium_interpolation.py`.
- **sum_assured:** confirmed separable from term (same ratio holds across every
  term within an age band) but **not simple proportional scaling** - checked
  numerically against the scraped CSV, not just eyeballed: Rs 1Cr costs ~1.64-1.68x
  of Rs 50L, not 2x; Rs 2Cr costs ~2.80-2.88x, not 4x. Tried deriving this from
  `rebate_structures.high_sum_assured_rebate_table`'s own percentages first (e.g.
  2 x (1-18%) = 1.64x) - close but didn't land exactly, implying an unmodeled
  fixed-cost component the rebate percentages alone don't capture. Decision: store
  the **empirical ratios directly**, keyed by age band (matching the rebate
  table's own `up_to_30`/`31_to_45` bands), rather than reverse-engineering them
  from the percentage table. Also surfaced a real accuracy gap in the
  **already-deployed** linear-in-SA scaling (`premium * SA/baseline_SA`) - it
  assumes exactly 2x/4x, a 16-28% overestimate at 1Cr/2Cr versus real quotes.

## Gap analysis before committing to bilinear, and one more scrape

Before building anything, checked how often a live query would actually have 4 real
corners to interpolate between. Naive check (nearest known age on each side,
requiring an exact match if the query age itself was a scraped anchor) showed 272/673
valid (age, term) combinations failing - but this was mostly an artifact of privileging
a *thin* anchor (ages 27/29/30 each have exactly one scraped term) over richer nearby
data. Fixed the bracket search to skip past a thin anchor to the next age that
actually has real term coverage, re-checked: **77/673 (11%) genuinely lacking a
usable box**, clustered in exactly two places:
1. Ages 18-19 - below the youngest scraped anchor (20), pure extrapolation zone.
2. Ages 36-44 at terms near that age's own maximum (bounded by `age_at_maturity_max`
   75) - **structural, not fixable by scraping**: e.g. age 44 can't take a 35-year
   term at all (44+35=79 > 75), so there's no real product combination to scrape
   there; the "gap" is the edge of the valid input domain itself, not missing data.

Prepped and the user scraped 6 more quotes (age 18, terms 15/20/25/30/35/40, DOB
15/01/2008, same convention as the existing 20/31/45 anchors) to close gap (1).
Ingested into `876_scraped_premiums.csv` (74 rows total now). Re-ran the gap check:
**25/673 (3.7%)** remaining, all in the structural maturity-edge band from (2) -
confirmed nothing left to scrape there.

## Built

- **`query/premium_lookup.py`** - the interpolation module: `bilinear_lookup()`
  (age x term, with the thin-anchor-skipping bracket search validated above),
  `sa_multiplier()` (piecewise-linear over the empirical age-band ratios),
  `lookup_premium()` combining both. Same return shape (`{"excluded": bool, ...}`)
  as the existing `interpolate_premium()`, so it's a drop-in at the call site.
- **`query/build_premium_lookup.py`** - builds `query/premium_lookup.json` by
  merging 876's Layer 1 brochure sample rows (3 points, all at the Rs 50L
  reference SA - confirmed by checking the raw JSON, not assumed) with the scraped
  CSV, deduping on an exact (age, term) collision in favor of the scraped value
  (already cross-validated identical where they overlap). Derives `sa_scaling` by
  averaging the premium ratio at each (age_band, sum_assured) across every term
  that has a sample - confirmed 2026-08 the ratio is constant across term within a
  band, so averaging smooths sampling noise rather than blending genuinely
  different values. Output: 41 age/term points, 4 sa_scaling points (2 SA tiers x
  2 age bands, n=5 to n=14 samples each).
- **`query/validate_premium_lookup.py`** - leave-one-out validation: hides each
  known point, re-predicts it from the rest, compares. **Median error 1.06%, mean
  3.53%** (35/41 points testable - 6 are isolated and can't be leave-one-out
  checked at all). One real outlier: age 31/term 15 at 64.78% error - not a defect,
  but a genuine finding that **term 15 is the thinnest slice of the grid** (only 4
  scraped ages: 18, 20, 31, 45), so removing its one mid-range anchor leaves a
  25-year gap that a straight line badly misjudges given the curve's real
  curvature. Flagged, not fixed this session - worth scraping 1-2 more term-15
  points (e.g. age 26, age 39) later, not blocking.
- **Wired into `query/premium_interpolation.py`**: `interpolate_premium()` now
  dispatches to the new lookup only when `policy_id == "876"` AND
  `premium_payment_option == "regular"` AND `sum_assured_type == "level"` (falls
  back to the original two-point linear interpolation otherwise, unchanged).
  Verified explicitly, not just assumed, that the scope guard actually holds:
  876/single and 876/increasing both still fall through to the old path; 877
  (untouched policy) still returns its old hand-checked values; the existing
  `__main__` hand-check suite in the file still passes unchanged, including its
  876-level end-to-end case, which now silently uses the new lookup and still
  returns the correct (brochure-matching) Rs 4700 for age 30/term 20.

## Session 2: corrected a mistaken conclusion - SA scaling needs zero scraping, not an empirical table

User asked directly whether the build script uses the rebate table at all, since
they can't scrape at 876's volume for every future policy and want scraping need
to shrink over time, not stay constant per policy. Prompted re-checking my
earlier claim that `rebate_structures.high_sum_assured_rebate_table`'s
percentages "don't land exactly" on the real scraped ratios (Session 1 above) -
that was wrong. Recomputed directly: `2x * (1 - 18%) = 1.64x` (up_to_30, Rs 1Cr)
and the other three combinations all match the real scraped ratios exactly. The
error was a band-boundary misreading - I'd assumed Rs 1Cr fell in the 0%-rebate
"50L_to_1Cr" band; it actually falls in "1Cr_to_2Cr" (bands are
lower-bound-inclusive, confirmed by checking which interpretation actually
reproduces the real data, not asserted).

**Rebuilt the SA-scaling axis around this correction:**
- `query/premium_lookup.py`'s `sa_multiplier()` now computes `(sum_assured /
  reference_sum_assured) x (1 - rebate_pct/100)` directly - a closed-form
  formula, not the empirical piecewise-linear-over-scraped-points table from
  Session 1.
- `query/build_premium_lookup.py` gained `load_rebate_bands()` (parses
  `high_sum_assured_rebate_table`'s SA-band key strings like `"50L_to_1Cr"` /
  `"5Cr_and_above"` into numeric bounds via a small Indian-amount parser) and
  rewrote `build_sa_scaling()` to emit those bands as the multiplier source,
  attaching the scraped CSV's ratios only as a `qa_check` cross-check per band
  (all 4 checks pass exactly against 876's real data) - the scrape no longer
  feeds the multiplier itself, only validates it.
- Real, concrete payoff verified directly: 876 can now price **any**
  sum_assured up to its real max (Rs 5Cr, via the open-ended `"and_above"`
  band), not just the Rs 1Cr/2Cr points that happened to get scraped -
  confirmed a Rs 3Cr and Rs 5Cr query that used to return `excluded: True` now
  resolve correctly. Re-ran the full `__main__` hand-check suite and
  `query/validate_premium_lookup.py` (age x term axis, untouched by this
  change) - both still pass unchanged.
- **This directly reduces future scraping need, which was the user's actual
  question:** once a policy's `rebate_structures` is real structured data (only
  876 so far - the other 6 term-assurance policies still hold the old
  prose-string placeholder, see the extraction-rule caveat), its SA axis needs
  zero scraping at all, ever - scraping is now purely a QA spot-check on the
  formula, as it should be.

**Open question raised, not yet tested:** does the age x term mortality-curve
*shape* (not the rebate percentages, which are confirmed policy-specific)
transfer across LIC term policies? Checked one related hypothesis this session
and it did NOT hold: `term` is not separable from `age` the way `sum_assured`
is - the term-40-vs-term-20 ratio drifts from ~1.33x at age 18 to ~1.86x at age
35 (real, monotonic, not sampling noise), ruling out a cheap rank-1
(age-factor x term-factor) decomposition of the whole surface. But whether the
full 2D *shape*, normalized, is similar across policies (as opposed to the
absolute premium level, which obviously differs) is still untested - would need
a small scrape (4-6 points, not a full grid) from one other policy (877
suggested) to check. If it holds, future policies' age x term surfaces could
lean on far less scraping than 876's 74 points; if not, each policy needs its
own curve as-is today. Logged as an open question in
`docs/query_architecture.md`, not acted on this session.
